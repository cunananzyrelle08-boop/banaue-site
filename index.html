Why It Matters

Carved by hands,
not machines.

Long before modern engineering, the Ifugao people shaped entire mountainsides into farmland — stone and mud walls climbing over 1,500 meters, fed by an ancient forest irrigation system called the muyong. Every terrace still grows heirloom Tinawon rice, harvested once a year, exactly as it was centuries ago.

The terraces are a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and one of the few living landscapes still farmed by the descendants of those who built it. But younger generations are leaving for the cities, and unmaintained walls collapse in the rainy season. What survives here survives because people keep choosing to return.

Location

Batad Amphitheater

An unbroken bowl of terraces climbing the mountainside, reachable only on foot. Homestays sit right along the rim, so the view is the first thing you see each morning and the last thing you see each night.

  • Best at sunrise
  • ~2 hr trek from Banaue proper
  • UNESCO core zone
Stay near Batad
Location

Sunrise Over Bangaan

A living village of traditional Ifugao huts, gathered like a nest at the foot of its own terrace cluster. Mist still pools in the valley at dawn, exactly the scene early travelers wrote home about.

  • Dawn light, 5:30–6:30 AM
  • 15 min from town center
  • Village homestays available
Stay near Bangaan
Food

Pinikpikan & Tinawon Rice

Every homestay meal is built around rice grown right outside the door. Pair it with pinikpikan, a smoked-chicken stew simmered with etag and mountain greens — cool-season comfort food at 1,500 meters.

  • Served at most homestays
  • Grown without machines
  • One harvest, once a year

Locals

Ifugao Elder

Sit with a host long enough and the terraces stop being scenery — they become family history. Many elders still recall which wall their grandfather rebuilt, and why, and when.

  • Storytelling in the evening
  • Weaving demonstrations
  • Ifugao & Filipino spoken
Meet the hosts
Location

Hapao Hot Springs

Natural springs tucked beside the Hungduan cluster, crossed by a hanging footbridge. After a full day of trekking, this is where sore legs go to recover, terrace views included.

  • Open most of the day
  • Combine with a Hungduan trek
  • Cluster elevation ~1,000m
Stay near Hungduan
Locals

Harvesting by Hand

Once a year, entire families move through the terraces cutting Tinawon rice stalk by stalk. Time a visit to the harvest season and a guide can bring you right into the fields to help — or just watch.

  • Seasonal — ask your host
  • A whole-village event
  • A favorite for photographers

Culture

Viewpoint Market

Rows of woven baskets, beadwork, and carved wooden bulul figures, sold by the families who make them. Bargain gently, ask about the pattern, and you'll usually get the story behind it too.

  • Open daily near the viewpoint
  • Buys support local weavers
  • Cash preferred

Find Your Way

Walk the contours.

Banaue sits at the heart of four inscribed clusters — Batad, Bangaan, Hungduan, and Mayoyao — linked by ridge trails and single-lane mountain roads. Trek between clusters over one to three days, staying in Ifugao-run homestays along the way.
